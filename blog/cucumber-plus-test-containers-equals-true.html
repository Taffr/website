Intro

Large tests, regressions, communication, and verifying behaviour

The dream of a well tested system; one that catches regressions, one that is easy to extend, one which inspires confidence in refactors, and who's test are never flaky is to many just that, a dream. It might seem so far away from reality that it is not even worth trying, or one might think that even starting to attempt to maintain such tests is a sisyphean; cursed to always push that boulder sized testing system, just for one flaky test to roll it back down the hill again.

In contrast, writing unit test seems trivial (given that you have a way of mocking away any nasty side effects from dependencies*), but for many applications without complicated operations or business logic, unit tests seem to not test more than "given the data exists, then I get the data", which seems trivial and like a waste of time, which I can sympathize with. Unit tests can also many times miss the large picture, everything works fine in isolation, but when a dependency changes which was mocked in other services, we can sometimes encounter a blind spot that is not spotted, and we end up with a regression. No, ideally in such cases we would want to pair our unit tests, with wider tests such as End-to-End tests.

End-to-End tests can be difficult to maintain, from my experience they are prone to "flakyness" ("A timeout here will surely fix this request sometimes taking a long time!"), and depending on your choice of testing (such as browser automation) can take a long time to develop. They also take quite a long time to run, making any effort to do TDD-like development feel a real chore.
